# Photo Poetic Postcard | Standalone Drawing Guide

Read this entire document, inspect the user's uploaded photo, and create one complete postcard with the original photograph above, one illustrated subject below, a Chinese title and short note, and three sampled color swatches.

This document works independently. Upload the photo and attach this MD file to an image-capable Agent, or paste its full text into the conversation. No Skill installation, project homepage, other file, code, or variable form is required. Make the visual decisions yourself when the user supplies no additional settings; apply explicit customization as described in section 13.

## 1. Two uses of the source photograph

The same photo serves two distinct visual purposes:

- **Original photographic area:** show the subject, setting, light, and spatial relationships recorded in the photograph. The environment belongs in this area and keeps its photographic appearance.
- **Source for the lower artwork:** choose one memorable subject, retain the features needed to recognize it, and express it through an artistic medium. Select the surrounding information carefully rather than carrying every tree, building, or river into this area.

The shared subject and source colors connect the two regions. They do not need to repeat the same complete setting or photographic framing.

Use only the uploaded photograph and information explicitly provided by the user as factual sources. Bridges, people, cats, and boats mentioned here illustrate selection decisions; never add them to an unrelated photo.

## 2. Observe before choosing the subject

Before generation, make these decisions internally; normally the user does not need to see the analysis:

1. Identify the object that attracts attention first. Observe its silhouette, scale, direction, pose, lighting, and characteristic colors.
2. Separate features that make it recognizable from things that merely happen to appear nearby.
3. Choose one concrete subject and make two brief lists: required features and environment to omit below.
4. Check reliable place evidence before composing the copy.
5. Arrange the lower subject, text, and swatches according to the subject's shape and visual weight.

Prefer a subject explicitly named by the user. Otherwise balance visual focus, distinctiveness, and the photo's theme. The largest area is not necessarily the subject: a small boat may be more meaningful than the surrounding sky.

When several objects have equal weight, choose the one that best represents the image on its own. An inseparable interaction may be treated as one compact subject unit, such as a kitten touching an ear of corn. This exception must not turn an entire street or forest into a supposed single subject.

For a landscape with no prominent object, select one distinctive rock formation, tree, or recognizable natural form. Ask which subject to emphasize only when the choice would materially change the user's intention and cannot reasonably be resolved.

## 3. Extract the subject and select information

Work in this order: observe and separate, choose a subject, retain identity, remove the environment, then reinterpret artistically.

### What to keep

Preserve the overall silhouette, important proportions, principal structure, pose, and representative colors. Select zero to three particularly useful identity cues, such as arches, roof tiers, a life jacket, or an interacting object.

The cue limit does not mean reducing a person's face or a building's structure to three strokes. The subject remains recognizable and may contain moderate material detail and watercolor variation.

For each supporting element, consider:

- Would removing it damage recognition or the essential action?
- Can it remain small and close to the subject without producing another scene?

Remove it if neither condition is met.

### What to omit

Create a concrete omission list using objects visible in this photo, such as the large tree on the right, distant forest, and broad river. “Simplify the background” alone is insufficient.

Normally remove unrelated sky, distant mountains, trees, streets, neighboring buildings, crowds, lamps, clutter, and broad ground surfaces. An object selected as the main subject is exempt from this environmental omission.

Extraction does not mean shrinking the photograph, lowering its opacity, blurring its background, or applying a watercolor filter. If the lower panel still reconstructs the original foreground, middle ground, and distance, too much environment has survived.

## 4. Decisions for different subjects

| Subject | Retain | Omit below | Acceptable small support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Old bridge | Bridge body, arch relationships, attached vines or moss | Forest, independent large trees, distant mountains, full river scene | A little water color or short reflection near the arches |
| Building, pavilion, temple | Main silhouette, roof rhythm, distinctive entrance or openings | Neighboring buildings, sky, plaza, tourists, distance | A small base directly supporting the structure |
| Person | Recognizable face, hair, clothing, important pose | Complete landscape or street setting | One local color wash; an essential held object |
| Animal | Face, coat colors, pose, body outline | Yard, walls, large floor area, clutter | A directly touching object essential to the action |
| Boat or vehicle | Body, distinctive structure, direction | Town, mountains, broad water or road | A tight reflection or short grounding mark |
| Flower ring, bouquet, installation | Overall shape, internal negative space, foliage, key colors | Lawn, lamps, distant facilities, sky | A necessary stand or faint color near the base |
| Natural landscape | One distinctive natural form or landform | Competing formations, broad sky, scattered scenery | A light wash close to the silhouette |

These are observation guides, not identical recipes for every building or person. A complex subject can carry richer internal detail; do not restore scenery merely to make it fuller.

## 5. Canvas and panel relationship [Canvas structure]

Default to an exact 3:4 portrait canvas divided horizontally into two equal, independent regions, 50% each. The upper region is photographic; the lower region is artwork on paper. Keep this clear division unless the user explicitly chooses another split.

For a 1080 × 1440 canvas, each region is 1080 × 720. This describes panel height, not an instruction to make the lower illustration fill all 720 pixels.

The panels meet directly with a clear boundary. Shared colors provide coherence; do not dissolve the photograph into an all-over watercolor with a broad gradient.

Generate the complete work in one image-generation operation, including the photograph, illustration, paper, text, swatches, and layout. Do not generate the bottom separately and stitch it to the photo, or add text and squares programmatically afterward.

## 6. Fit the photograph [Upper panel — source photo lock]

Fill the entire upper region, flush with the canvas top, left, and right edges. No paper margin, white bars, outline, frame, rounded corners, or shadow.

Upper-panel zero margins and lower-panel breathing room are separate rules. For a default canvas of width W and height H, photography covers the entire rectangle from left to right and from the canvas top to H/2. Its bottom edge meets the panel boundary directly, without a paper strip or separator stroke. This is not a smaller photograph mounted on paper. Paper texture and artistic whitespace belong only to the lower panel, never an ivory outer frame around the complete work.

Inspect the three outer photo edges and the panel boundary. Any paper gap between the photograph and the canvas top, left, or right edge, or any thin line framing the photograph, fails the design even if the lower illustration is correct.

Preserve photographic subjects, lighting, colors, spatial relationships, and character. The lower medium must not stylize the upper photograph. Add no title, note, swatches, or decoration above.

When the source and photo-region ratios differ:

- Scale proportionally until the region is covered, then position the crop around the essential subject.
- For wide architecture, prioritize the roof and distinctive outer structure. For a portrait, prioritize the face, essential gesture, and important clothing.
- Do not stretch faces, flatten buildings, or invent missing scenery at the sides.
- If the required subject cannot fit, explain the crop conflict and offer a concrete change to photo share or canvas ratio. Do not silently stretch, extend, or introduce oversized side margins.

Faithfulness means preserving visible source content. It does not mean that every source edge can survive any fixed output ratio. Crop only as needed and disclose an unresolved conflict.

## 7. Medium and detail [Lower panel — one extracted subject]

Reinterpret only the selected subject. Default to transparent watercolor with a little light gouache and necessary colored-pencil structure, retaining identity, depth, and key colors.

- Use transparent layers, natural bleeding edges, and restrained wet/dry variation.
- Use gouache sparingly for local volume or highlights rather than heavy opaque masses.
- Use pencil to explain important structure, not every brick, leaf, or twig.
- Concentrate detail where recognition requires it; simplify peripheral areas and let edges dissolve into paper.
- If the source already cuts off the subject, retain a natural partial view or fade the crop edge. Do not invent unseen structures or limbs merely to complete a silhouette.

Richness should come from the subject's own material, light, detail, and brushwork. If too sparse, add modest internal detail or a close grounding cue. If too full, remove the environment first, then reduce the subject and wash.

A user-requested ink, printmaking, pencil, or other medium changes the lower treatment while preserving subject selection and panel roles.

## 8. Scale, position, and breathing room

All sizes refer to the lower panel. Treat the following as starting points and judge width, height, and visual weight together:

- Bridges and wide buildings usually occupy about 55%–70% of lower width while remaining relatively low.
- People, animals, and compact objects usually occupy about 40%–55%. Do not force a tall subject to meet a width target at the expense of its top and bottom clearance.
- Simple rings, symbols, or installations with large internal holes should start smaller. Their outer contour still has visual weight even when the center is empty.
- Detailed architecture can retain more internal information while leaving space around its silhouette, text, and swatches.

Keep at least about 45% of the lower region visibly quiet by default. Quiet paper is continuous, low-contrast space without scenery. A dense watercolor background made pale does not count as clean breathing room. This is a visual target; do not pretend to have measured a segmentation mask.

The subject may sit left, center, right, slightly high, or slightly low. Leave clearance on all sides; roughly 5% of lower width is a useful starting inset. Preserve room for readable copy and at least one continuous area containing no visual element.

## 9. Paper, wash, and palette

Default to warm, low-saturation ivory paper below, with fine, subdued fibers. Avoid heavy grain, dirt, and large stains. Do not place a paper-texture filter over the upper photo.

Select colors actually present in the source: a principal subject color for recognition, a deeper tone for structure and copy, and a lighter supporting tone. Gentle desaturation is allowed; do not make every photo use the same green or orange palette.

Use at most one localized, soft, translucent, irregular wash behind the subject. It may be omitted entirely.

- Keep it near the subject and let its edges retreat into paper.
- Do not form a rectangular image backing, complete circular disk, or large opaque geometric shape.
- Do not paint trees, clouds, mountains, buildings, or a second focal point inside it.
- Reduce its extent or strength if it occupies the text area or obscures the subject.

## 10. Place evidence and copy

Determine facts before writing. Use this evidence order:

1. A place or landmark name explicitly provided by the user.
2. Clearly readable source signage, or EXIF/geolocation data actually available to the host.
3. A uniquely recognizable public landmark identified with high confidence.

Do not assume every upload retains location metadata. Similar bridges or mountain shapes do not establish an exact place. Verify externally when needed and available; otherwise do not guess.

A confirmed place's exact name must appear in either the title or note. A long name may go in the note; do not remove meaningful parts to meet a character target. A plaque establishes only the name it shows, not an unverified city or larger attraction.

Without a confirmed place, compose from visible subjects, actions, light, or mood. A Chinese title is usually 3–6 characters and its note usually 6–10; accuracy and natural language take priority over mechanical length. The note should add a different observation, not repeat the title or invent a story.

Preserve user-supplied wording exactly. This English guide also defaults to Chinese artwork copy; switch languages only when requested. Choose suitable copy yourself when unspecified, without asking the user to fill a title template.

## 11. Typography and copy placement

Default to a clear contemporary Song-style Chinese serif, with a larger title and lighter note in one compact group. Architecture may suit a steadier face and nature a lighter humanist treatment. Use handwriting only when requested or when legibility allows.

Choose a deeper source-derived text color with sufficient contrast against the paper, not automatically the brightest accent.

Place copy according to the illustration's silhouette:

- If the subject sits right, inspect the open left side first.
- If it sits left, inspect the open right side.
- For a wide subject in the upper-middle of the lower panel, copy may sit below at left, right, or center.
- For a tall central subject, consider side-upper or side-lower space instead of squeezing everything along the bottom.
- A centered layout is valid when balanced; asymmetry is not compulsory.

Keep all copy inside the lower region, clear of the subject and canvas edges. For longer copy, adjust line breaks, size, and surrounding space; do not distort glyphs or enlarge the entire lower panel to accommodate it.

## 12. Three swatches and adaptive placement [Typography and swatches]

Choose three representative source colors that are visibly distinguishable. They may correspond to the principal subject, supporting environment, and dark structure. A monochrome source can use three distinct grays.

The swatches are a minor palette annotation:

- Exactly three equal, perfect squares in a compact horizontal row.
- Each side is approximately 1/20 of lower-panel width.
- Each fill is 100% uniform and flat, without paper texture, patterns, gradients, photo fragments, text labels, or shadows.
- Do not turn sampled colors into miniature photographs.
- No circles, irregular shapes, or fourth square.

Place the group in any sufficiently open corner of the lower panel, inset from its edges. Upper-left and upper-right here mean near the top of the lower panel, never the photographic corners of the whole image.

Place the subject and copy first, then choose among the remaining corners. When several work equally well, select randomly; reconsider if the choice causes collision or imbalance. Across different photos, vary the subject, copy, and swatches naturally.

## 13. User-adjustable choices

Explicit user choices override defaults. Resolve unspecified settings through observation.

| Choice | Adaptation | Retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aspect ratio or pixel size | Recompose for the requested dimensions | One complete work; no distorted ratio |
| Panel share | Recalculate the two heights | Photography above, extracted subject below |
| Illustration style | Change medium, strokes, and detail density | Subject identity and structure |
| Subject scale and offset | Make it smaller, airier, or moderately larger | Clear space outside the silhouette |
| Paper and color mood | Warm/cool white, smooth/fiber paper, modest color adjustment | No corresponding art filter over the photograph |
| Title, note, language, type | Use exact requested copy; omit note or all text if requested | Readability and lower-only placement when shown |
| Swatches | Reposition, resample, reduce size, or hide | Three equal solid squares when shown |
| Lower arrangement | Rebalance illustration, copy, and palette | No overlap or competing focus |

When the user requests no text or no swatches, disable the corresponding defaults and checks. The resulting space may stay empty; do not restore the full scene to fill it. A request for another language also overrides the Chinese-only default.

## 14. Execution in different Agents

A host that can inspect the photo and generate from a reference image can use this document directly. No repository installation, code execution, or exclusive model is required.

If you call a separate image tool, turn your observations into complete, specific drawing instructions, preserving every applicable requirement without summarizing away the subject, omission list, split, copy, palette, or whitespace. Attach the original photo. You may resolve general guidance into image-specific decisions; tool-capability explanations should not appear in the artwork.

Use separate sections for [Canvas structure], [Upper panel — source photo lock], [Lower panel — one extracted subject], [Typography and swatches], and [Prohibitions and delivery]. Populate each with concrete decisions; do not send empty headings or depend on another file.

- **Vision and generation:** analyze and generate directly. Do not require user approval of a prompt by default.
- **Vision only:** return a complete image-specific drawing instruction and explain that it needs a reference-image generator. Repeating this generic document is not completed photo analysis.
- **No vision:** explain the missing capability without inventing a subject or place. Use an image-capable host or a user description; a description does not replace the reference photo for photographic fidelity.
- **Input-length limit:** do not silently truncate. Resolve relevant guidance into concrete decisions while preserving all active constraints. If it still does not fit, disclose the limit and supply the full instruction for another entry point.

When real size, ratio, or image-count parameters are available, set them consistently with the text. Do not claim an unavailable setting was applied.

## 15. Inspect and correct [Prohibitions and delivery]

Check composition before fine detail:

1. Actual aspect ratio matches the request; default 3:4 is not approximately 2:3. Read dimensions when possible; do not claim exact verification without access.
2. Panel boundary and split are clear; default 50/50, with a full-bleed borderless upper photo.
3. The lower area contains only the selected subject and necessary attached cues; concrete omitted background objects did not return.
4. Scale is balanced and clean paper remains around the subject; it is not a smaller complete landscape painting.
5. Confirmed place names appear and unconfirmed locations were not invented.
6. Text is exact, readable, and entirely below.
7. Swatch count, shape, size, fill, and placement are correct, or absent when requested.
8. There is no extra decoration, instructional copy, logo, signature, watermark, frame, or candidate grid.

Correct the cause: remove restored scenery for a full-scene repeat; reduce the subject and wash for crowding; add internal subject detail for sparseness; reposition copy when cramped; enforce uniform fill when swatches resemble fragments.

Corrections must still regenerate or edit the complete work through image generation, never programmatic stitching, cropping of the finished art, or text overlays. If the tool persistently cannot satisfy the request, state the failed criterion rather than claiming compliance or generating indefinitely.

## 16. Final delivery

By default, one source photo produces one complete postcard. Photography, illustration, paper, copy, swatches, and layout belong to the same generated work.

Do not proactively request multiple styles or a four-up grid. If the host automatically returns several images and selection is available, show one compliant result. If the platform preview cannot be controlled, explain that accurately.

For a normal successful request, show the finished image without internal analysis, title candidates, parameters, or document headings. Explain the design briefly only when asked.
